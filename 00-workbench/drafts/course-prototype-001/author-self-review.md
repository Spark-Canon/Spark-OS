Both files are written. Now the required validation pass before reporting back.

**Content lens.** Claims are either bracketed placeholders or explicitly labeled prototype assumptions — nothing states a specific figure as fact. The risk isn't inaccuracy so much as the *shape* of the taxonomy itself being unverified; I flagged that distinctly in Findings rather than letting the placeholders create false confidence that everything else is solid.

**Philosophy lens.** Represents uncertainty honestly (every unconfirmed figure is visible, not smoothed over) — consistent with Represent Reality Honestly. Supports informed choice indirectly by teaching the agent what still needs verifying before a client hears anything definite. Preserves accountability by refusing to resolve the escalation question on Spark's behalf (Question 5, Case 5) rather than quietly inventing a threshold. Doesn't claim to manufacture competence — stated explicitly in the brief and outcomes section. One place I had to be deliberate: Match Readiness to Consequence shows up structurally (Case 5 exists specifically to model "stop and verify" over "guess"), not just as a citation.

**Learning lens.** The two structural-gate cases (3 and 4) are doing real work — they're the only way to actually test "Understand" rather than "Know," since a learner could recall the three names without grasping that down payment isn't dispositive. Question 5 is deliberately the least scaffolded so it tests transfer, not pattern-matching against the Apply cases. Alternate defensible answers are named explicitly rather than papered over (Question 5, Case 1's hedged phrasing).

**Operational lens.** Real risk: the banner and non-authoritative framing are clear, but a busy new agent skimming for "the answer" could still walk away treating the general category logic as settled fact rather than noticing every specific number is bracketed. That's a real mistaking-the-prototype-for-training risk, not a hypothetical one. Everything numeric needs SME sign-off before this moves past Workbench status — that's the dominant maintenance cost, and it's ongoing (thresholds drift externally), not a one-time review.
