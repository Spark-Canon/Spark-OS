## Final Conclusion

The prototype's `insured`, `insurable`, and `uninsurable` model does not survive as an exhaustive general taxonomy of Canadian residential mortgages.

Its most durable domain insight is narrower:

> LTV alone cannot determine insurance eligibility, actual insurance status, premium responsibility, lender availability, pricing, documentation, or the appropriate operational response.

If the three labels are used later, they must be explicitly defined as bounded Spark or industry shorthand after the underlying facts have been established. They must not replace dimensional analysis.

## Corrected Scenario Dispositions

### Case 1 — Standard high-ratio purchase

The broad direction toward required mortgage-default insurance is defensible, subject to current borrower, property, value, debt-service, insurer, and lender criteria. The prototype identified the wrong remaining uncertainties for some current thresholds.

### Case 2 — Low-ratio owner-occupied purchase

Low-ratio status is clear from the stated LTV. `Insurable`, actual insurance, insurance channel, and premium responsibility are not established from the supplied facts.

### Case 3 — Debt-consolidation refinance

Current purpose-limited secondary-suite programs disprove a universal rule that every refinance is insurance-ineligible. They do not establish ordinary debt-consolidation refinance as eligible.

The defensible finding is that the reviewed public sources did not establish an insured pathway for the frozen debt-consolidation case. This is not proof of universal `uninsurability` across every insurer, lender, funding, or portfolio channel.

Equity and LTV remain material. Refinance purpose is also material.

### Case 4 — Non-owner-occupied rental purchase

The frozen scenario omits unit count.

- Current reviewed products establish pathways for qualifying non-owner-occupied two-to-four-unit purchases.
- The same reviewed products do not establish those pathways for single-unit non-owner-occupied purchases.
- Therefore the scenario is underdetermined.
- The prototype's universal rationale that non-owner occupancy independently disqualifies every rental purchase is contradicted.

Do not infer a one-unit property from the singular word `property`; a duplex, triplex, or fourplex is also one property.

### Case 5 — Owner-occupied duplex

Owner occupancy of one unit is contemplated by current federal high-ratio criteria. The occupancy structure is not inherently disqualifying. Actual value, income treatment, insurer product, lender overlay, and other underwriting facts remain material.

A two-year self-employment requirement found in one Canada Guaranty product is product-specific and cannot be generalized.

## Decision Dimensions

The following dimensions survived review as a candidate minimum set:

1. Transaction purpose and type.
2. Occupancy and intended use.
3. Property type and unit count.
4. LTV, value, and amortization.
5. Applicable federal criteria.
6. Insurer and product eligibility.
7. Lender overlay and channel availability.
8. Actual insurance status and insurance channel.
9. Premium and surcharge responsibility.

These are working distinctions, not an accepted ontology or schema.

## Candidate Decision Sequence

1. Establish transaction purpose.
2. Establish occupancy, use, property type, and unit count.
3. Establish LTV, value, amortization, and other material facts.
4. Identify applicable federal criteria.
5. Identify the relevant insurer and product pathway.
6. Check lender access, overlays, and channel conditions.
7. Confirm whether insurance is required, optional, obtained, unavailable, or unresolved.
8. Identify premium or surcharge responsibility.
9. Record uncertainty and verify or escalate proportionately before communicating a settled conclusion.

The sequence must be tested by a practitioner. It is not operating policy.

## Evidence Gates

### Gate A — Bounded Workbench correction

Gate A may identify and deprecate factually unsupported definitions, universal conclusions, classifications, and answer keys using current public evidence.

Gate A does not require complete lender-panel, pricing, documentation, escalation, or learner-behaviour evidence. Those gaps must remain explicit.

Gate A does not authorize changing the frozen PR #12 prototype. Any future correction must be a clearly identified successor artifact.

### Gate B — Learner-facing or operational use

Before Canon, Academy, training, workflow, advice, or operational use, Spark must additionally establish the relevant:

- lender-panel availability and overlays;
- pricing, premium, and documentation treatment;
- current practitioner terminology;
- independent-verification and escalation boundaries;
- valid replacement assessments;
- learner-performance claims;
- human approval and repository acceptance.

Removing or marking known false answers is a Gate A activity. Designing and validating replacement learner assessments is a Gate B activity.
