# Licensed Practitioner Review Brief — Course Prototype 001

**Status:** Non-authoritative Workbench review brief

**Purpose:** Operational validation only

**Repository impact:** None authorized

## Reviewer

The substantive reviewer must be a licensed and currently practising Canadian mortgage professional familiar with the intended jurisdiction and a representative portion of Spark Mortgages' lender access.

AI may organize notes and format the report. AI cannot substitute for the practitioner's judgment or certify current practice.

Record:

- reviewer name or approved role identifier;
- licensing jurisdiction and current-practice confirmation; no licence number is required in a public artifact;
- review date, relevant experience, and lender-channel scope;
- material limitations or conflicts;
- whether AI assisted only with organization or wording.

## Objective

Test whether the corrected domain model reflects current practice through Spark-accessible lenders, insurers, submission channels, and workflows.

Determine whether a future successor should:

1. use separate dimensions through a decision sequence;
2. retain the three traditional labels only as defined shorthand;
3. omit one or more labels; or
4. abandon the content framing while preserving Prototype 001 as process evidence.

Do not draft the successor or make final Spark policy.

## Information Safety

Do not provide or preserve:

- credentials or authentication information;
- client or application information;
- portal screenshots;
- proprietary rate sheets or restricted documents;
- confidential communications;
- copied access-controlled text or tables.

For restricted evidence, record only the source owner, source type, effective or observed date, access classification, proposition tested, sanitized result, reviewer confirmation, and limitation.

Do not paste restricted evidence into AI unless permitted. Do not place restricted material in GitHub. Use `privately reviewed — access-limited` when no public citation is available.

## Evidence Dispositions

Use:

- **Confirmed — public source**
- **Confirmed — access-limited practitioner evidence**
- **Varies by insurer, lender, product, channel, or date**
- **Contradicted**
- **Not verified**
- **Outside review scope**

## Candidate Dimensions

Test whether these are distinct and sufficient:

1. Transaction purpose and type.
2. Occupancy and intended use.
3. Property type and unit count.
4. LTV, value, and amortization.
5. Applicable federal criteria.
6. Insurer and product eligibility.
7. Lender overlay and channel availability.
8. Actual insurance status and channel.
9. Premium and surcharge responsibility.

Add a dimension only when its absence repeatedly changes a consequential file decision. Do not build a comprehensive ontology.

## Candidate Sequence

Test:

1. Transaction purpose.
2. Occupancy, use, property type, and unit count.
3. LTV, value, amortization, and material borrower/property facts.
4. Federal criteria.
5. Insurer and product pathway.
6. Lender access, overlays, and channel conditions.
7. Required, optional, obtained, unavailable, or unresolved insurance.
8. Premium and surcharge responsibility.
9. Document uncertainty and verify or escalate before communicating a settled conclusion.

Report where real practice branches or requires reordered steps. Do not make the sequence mandatory policy.

## Scenario Tests

Use fictional or generic facts only:

1. Standard owner-occupied high-ratio purchase.
2. Low-ratio purchase with no known insurance in force.
3. Low-ratio purchase that may be transactionally, portfolio, or bulk insured.
4. Owner-occupied duplex or triplex with rental income.
5. Non-owner-occupied two-to-four-unit rental purchase.
6. Non-owner-occupied single-unit rental purchase.
7. Qualifying second or seasonal home.
8. Ordinary debt-consolidation or equity-takeout refinance.
9. Purpose-limited secondary-suite refinance.
10. One mixed-use, insurer-declined, or lender-overlay case.

For each record terminology, material dimensions, evidence checked, possible pathways, lender variation, unresolved facts, independent-verification options, example escalation boundary, and what must not be presented to a client as settled.

## Review Questions

### Terminology and workflow

1. How do Spark-accessible lenders, insurer portals, commitments, and rate sheets use the relevant terms?
2. Do meanings vary materially between actors?
3. Is `uninsurable` useful enough to retain when actor, product, and channel must be named?
4. Would shorthand improve or weaken developing-agent judgment?

### Product and lender reality

1. Which public insurer pathways are accessible through a representative part of Spark's panel?
2. Which lender overlays narrow published eligibility?
3. Does any relevant channel cover ordinary debt-consolidation or equity-takeout refinance?
4. How are the rental, multi-unit, second-home, mixed-use, and secondary-suite scenarios treated?

### Pricing, premium, and documentation

1. Does insurance status materially affect pricing after other factors are separated?
2. How are premiums and surcharges represented in commitments or workflows?
3. Which documentation differences arise from insurance status versus borrower, property, income, transaction, insurer, or lender requirements?
4. Report directionally when exact material is restricted.

### Developing-agent judgment

1. Which facts can be verified through approved current sources?
2. Which ambiguities require an experienced reviewer?
3. What errors have actually been observed? Separate observation from measured evidence.
4. Provide operational evidence, not final escalation policy.

### Learning scope

1. What belongs in introductory material?
2. What belongs in advanced material, supervised practice, or reference material?
3. Can shorthand be introduced safely before the dimensions?
4. Does the candidate sequence reflect real file work?
