It does not create mortgage Canon, Academy content, a comprehensive schema, a database, an automation, an authoritative sourcing standard, or a course-development process.

## Existing Constraints

Current Canon Governance already establishes that canonical material should be accurate, scoped, internally consistent, traceable when facts may change, reusable, explicit about uncertainty and exceptions, and separated from teaching activities.

Accepted Philosophy adds that Spark should:

- represent material matters honestly according to the best evidence it can reasonably obtain;
- make the material basis, assumptions, interpretation, uncertainty, and limits of consequential judgment clear;
- reconsider consequential conclusions when credible material evidence warrants it;
- preserve the provenance and context future judgment may require; and
- act proportionately rather than demanding exhaustive process for every claim.

The accepted Glossary also prevents several shortcuts:

- evidence is relevant material, not automatic proof of truth, sufficiency, authority, or currency;
- judgment includes contextual evaluation and cannot be replaced by a source or automated output;
- materiality identifies possible significance but does not dictate maximum process; and
- proportionality does not override binding requirements or determine which source governs.

## Working Authority Model

Canon acceptance does not transfer ownership of an external rule or policy to Spark. It records what Spark has accepted about that source and how it applies.

| Knowledge class | Legitimate underlying owner | What Spark may accept into Canon | What acceptance cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| External legal or regulatory requirement | Applicable legislature, regulator, court, or other legitimate external authority | A scoped, current representation of the requirement and its applicable interpretation | Amend the external rule or make Spark's summary legally authoritative |
| External insurer, lender, vendor, or product rule | The issuing organization, subject to superior obligations | A scoped representation of the published or authorized rule and its known operational applicability | Prove access, approval, pricing, or universal market practice beyond the evidence |
| Spark organizational decision or standard | The authorized Spark decision owner under Governance | The accepted internal choice, its scope, rationale, and relationship to external constraints | Present the local choice as an external requirement |
| Professional or operational synthesis | No source owns the synthesis; qualified evidence and judgment support it | A bounded Spark interpretation when evidence, limits, practitioner input, and acceptance justify reliance | Turn practitioner opinion, repetition, or confidence into external authority |
| Research synthesis | Research sources plus transparent synthesis | Evidence that may inform a later Canon decision | Become Canon merely because it is credible, current, or AI-produced |
| Teaching simplification | Academy after underlying Canon exists | Normally a derivative explanation linked to Canon | Create or replace the domain truth it teaches |

## Claim Tests

### C01 — External regulatory criterion

**Why it matters:** A learner or advisor could use this claim to avoid incorrectly treating a multi-unit owner-occupied property as categorically ineligible.

**What the current evidence can show:** The preserved review reports that current federal criteria contemplate owner occupancy of one unit. It also identifies an official regulatory source.

**What is still missing for Canon admission:**

- the exact provision or official pinpoint supporting the statement;
- confirmation that the provision remains in force and applies to the intended transaction context;
- the relevant property, unit-count, loan-to-value, value, amortization, borrower, and insurer boundaries;
- separation of the federal criterion from insurer and lender overlays; and
- review by a competent person where legal or regulated interpretation is consequential.

**Admission lesson:** An official source name is insufficient. The claim must be narrow enough that a reviewer can confirm the source supports the complete statement rather than a nearby or broader proposition.

**Provisional result:** `Hold — potentially admissible after current primary-source verification and scope definition.`

### C02 — Insurer product rule

**Why it matters:** The claim corrects the prototype's false universal inference that non-owner occupancy independently makes every rental purchase insurance-ineligible.

**What the current evidence can show:** The preserved review reports qualifying insurer pathways for non-owner-occupied two-to-four-unit properties and identifies relevant insurer products.

**What it cannot show without more evidence:**

- that every insurer offers the pathway;
- that the pathway applies to a one-unit rental property;
- that Spark has lender-panel or channel access;
- that a lender will approve the file;
- current pricing, premiums, documentation, or overlays; or
- that published eligibility equals operational availability.

**Admission lesson:** Product-issued material can be authoritative for the issuer's published product description while remaining insufficient for market-wide or Spark-access claims. One Canon entry may need to preserve those distinctions rather than compress them into one conclusion.

**Provisional result:** `Hold — admissible only as a dated, issuer- and product-scoped representation after current verification.`

### C03 — Proposed Spark operating practice

**Why it matters:** The practice could protect clients and developing agents when a classification remains uncertain.

**Evidence role:** External sources and practitioners may inform whether the practice is workable, but they cannot decide Spark's local responsibility, delegation, or escalation rule. That requires an authorized Spark decision consistent with external obligations and accepted Philosophy.

**What is still missing:**

- the role and delegated authority to which the practice applies;
- what information the person can reasonably verify;
- which consequences require escalation;
- available supervision and response time;
- applicable compliance or lender requirements; and
- a human decision accepting the practice.

**Admission lesson:** Not every Canon entry needs an external citation as its authority. A Spark policy needs explicit internal decision provenance and must be labeled as a Spark choice rather than presented as an industry rule.

**Provisional result:** `Hold — candidate internal standard pending practitioner evidence, operating context, and human acceptance.`

### C04 — Professional/domain synthesis

**Why it matters:** This is the prototype review's most durable correction. It warns against using one variable as a complete operational classification.

**Evidence role:** Multiple counterexamples and distinctions support the synthesis. No single source is likely to contain this exact sentence because the conclusion compares several rules, products, actors, and operational consequences.

**What is still missing:**

- a claim-to-source map showing which part of the synthesis each source supports;
- current practitioner confirmation of operational relevance;
- explicit scope, including whether the statement applies across all Spark-accessible residential channels;
- any valid circumstances in which LTV alone can settle a narrower question; and
- a clear distinction between observed evidence and Spark's accepted reasoning model.

**Admission lesson:** Canon must be able to accept a transparent synthesis without pretending it is a quotation or direct source finding. The comparative basis, assumptions, and limits must remain visible.

**Provisional result:** `Hold — strongest candidate for the later bounded Canon pilot after practitioner review.`

### C05 — Teaching simplification

**Why it matters:** The three labels produced a clear lesson and apparently decisive answer keys. They also compressed transaction purpose, occupancy, property type, LTV, federal criteria, insurer pathways, lender access, insurance status, and premium responsibility into one apparent taxonomy.

**Contrary evidence:** The preserved domain review found that the categories do not survive as an exhaustive general taxonomy and documented scenarios the model classified incorrectly or could not determine from the stated facts.

**Admission lesson:** Memorability, course completion, repetition, subject-matter familiarity, and internal consistency do not establish Canon. A simplification that hides dimensions material to the conclusion should be rejected, narrowed, or kept only as explicitly bounded shorthand after the domain basis is established.

**Provisional result:** `Reject in exhaustive form. Do not use as a Canon model or learner-facing answer system.`

## Provisional Minimum Admission Test

A proposed Canon claim is not ready unless a reviewer can answer all applicable questions below.

1. **Claim:** What exact proposition or instruction is Spark being asked to accept?
2. **Intended reliance:** What decision, explanation, workflow, reference, AI context, or teaching dependency may rely on it?
3. **Knowledge class and authority:** Is it an external requirement, issuer rule, Spark decision, bounded synthesis, or another clearly identified class, and who legitimately owns the underlying authority?
4. **Evidence support:** What retrievable evidence supports the claim, and does it support the complete wording rather than a narrower, adjacent, or historical statement?
5. **Scope and applicability:** To which jurisdiction, actor, product, channel, transaction, population, conditions, and exclusions does it apply?
6. **Basis of judgment:** What is directly sourced, synthesized, inferred, interpreted, or deliberately chosen by Spark?
7. **Uncertainty and contrary evidence:** What material limits, disagreements, inaccessible evidence, exceptions, or warning signs remain?
8. **Currency:** What effective or as-of conditions apply, and what event or evidence should trigger review?
9. **Acceptance:** Who may accept the entry, what status will it have, and are any professional, legal, privacy, confidentiality, or access constraints unresolved?

Failure does not always mean rejection. The correct result may be to narrow the claim, keep it in the Workbench, record it as unresolved, seek better evidence, obtain practitioner review, or identify a Spark decision that has not yet been made.

## Provisional Minimum Entry Fields

The cases suggest nine fields. These are information requirements, not a final Markdown template or schema.

1. **Entry name** — a stable human-readable identifier.
2. **Accepted statement** — the exact knowledge Spark accepts, written narrowly enough to test.
3. **Knowledge class** — external requirement, issuer rule, Spark standard, bounded synthesis, or another defined class.
4. **Authority** — the legitimate external owner and/or Spark acceptance owner, without conflating them.
5. **Scope and applicability** — jurisdiction, actors, products, channels, transactions, conditions, and material exclusions.
6. **Basis and provenance** — sources, precise locations, evidence relationship, and separation of direct support from synthesis or interpretation.
7. **Uncertainty and exceptions** — material limitations, disagreement, unresolved application, and known contrary evidence.
8. **Currency and review trigger** — effective or as-of context plus the event, date, dependency change, or contrary evidence that should prompt review.
9. **Acceptance and dependencies** — approval status, decision owner, related Canon, and downstream materials that may require review when the entry changes.

`Not applicable` may be valid for a field when the reason is clear. The standard should not force invented metadata merely to complete a form.

## Focused Challenge Cases

| Case | Required treatment under the provisional test |
|---|---|
| Official but stale source | It may support a historical point-in-time claim, but not a current claim until currency is re-established. Authority does not guarantee currency. |
| Current but access-controlled lender material | It may support a scoped entry only when authorized reviewers can verify it, the access restriction is recorded, and protected content is not improperly reproduced. An inaccessible source cannot be treated as publicly verifiable. |
| Conflicting qualified interpretations | Record the disagreement, evidence, material consequences, and current Spark decision or unresolved status. Do not manufacture consensus or treat all positions as equally supported. |
| Spark practice stricter than an external minimum | Record the external requirement and the Spark standard separately. The Spark choice must not be described as law, regulation, or universal industry practice. |
| Plausible AI synthesis without retrievable support | Treat it as a research lead or candidate reasoning, not evidence sufficient for Canon. Correctness cannot be verified from persuasiveness. |
| One issuer's product page used for a market-wide claim | Narrow to that issuer and product or obtain adequate independent evidence for the broader statement. |
| Practitioner consensus without a public primary source | Treat practitioner evidence as scoped operational evidence. Record who was consulted, recency, panel or channel limits, disagreements, and the distinction from external authority. |
| Fixed fictional scenario with an answer key | The fictional facts need no external proof, but the classification, rationale, feedback, and expected reasoning are domain claims and must derive from accepted Canon. |

The provisional test survives these cases, but the access-controlled-source and practitioner-evidence treatments require focused Challenge before adoption.

## Initial Findings

1. **The unit of admission must be the claim, not merely the document.** One document can contain sourced facts, synthesis, Spark decisions, examples, and unresolved material with different authority.
2. **Source authority, repository authority, and operational applicability are separate.** An insurer can authoritatively describe its product without establishing Spark's lender access or a lender's approval.
3. **Source count is not a sufficiency standard.** One direct legitimate source may be enough for a narrow claim; several weak or mutually dependent sources may remain inadequate.
4. **Primary sources should be preferred when they own the rule, but not made a universal ritual.** Some Spark decisions and operational syntheses require different evidence.
5. **A source does not eliminate judgment.** Scope, applicability, conflict, currency, and materiality still require accountable evaluation.
6. **Acceptance must preserve classification.** External fact, Spark decision, synthesis, and teaching simplification should not be flattened into identical-looking assertions.
7. **Currency should use context and triggers, not a universal expiry period.** Volatility, consequence, source changes, and downstream dependency determine review need.
8. **Canon admission and Academy derivation are distinct gates.** An accepted domain statement may still require instructional simplification, examples, assessments, and practitioner validation before teaching.
9. **Research evidence is not Canon.** The Training Research standard supplies useful evidence distinctions but explicitly does not establish a Canon acceptance standard.
10. **The minimum standard should enable bounded refusal.** `Hold`, `narrow`, `unresolved`, and `reject` are valid outcomes; completeness pressure must not convert missing evidence into confident prose.

## Deferred or Rejected Design Choices

The following are not justified during Explore:

- a universal minimum number of sources;
- mandatory corroboration for every claim;
- a universal primary-source-only rule;
- a fixed review or expiry period for every entry;
- numerical confidence scores;
- one comprehensive domain ontology;
- database, YAML, JSON, or front-matter schema decisions;
- automated downstream synchronization or semantic CI;
- a source archive or evidence-library architecture;
- a separate Canon status taxonomy beyond what actual use demonstrates;
- mortgage-domain folders before accepted content requires them; or
- treating the nine provisional fields as a final template.

## Questions for Challenge

1. Can the nine admission questions or nine entry fields be reduced without losing a boundary demonstrated by the five claims?
2. Should intended reliance be stored in each entry, or is it a review question that belongs outside Canon?
3. Should knowledge class be a required field, or can headings and wording preserve the distinction adequately?
4. How should a Canon entry cite access-controlled lender or insurer material without exposing restricted information or making the basis unverifiable?
5. What evidence makes practitioner observation adequate for a bounded operational claim, and when is broader or independent review necessary?
6. When should a synthesis remain a linked reasoning record rather than appear as the primary Canon statement?
7. Which currency information belongs at entry level versus source level?
8. Are approval identity, date, and status required inside every entry, or can repository Governance and Git history supply part of that provenance?
9. How should downstream dependencies be preserved without creating a manually maintained map that becomes stale?
10. Does the standard distinguish sufficiently between factual acceptance and Spark choosing a local rule?

## Current Disposition

**Explore complete enough for focused Challenge; the exploration remains active and non-authoritative.**

The five-claim exercise supports a provisional nine-question admission test and nine information fields. It also demonstrates that Canon sourcing is not merely a citation format: it must preserve claim granularity, authority class, applicability, evidence relationship, currency, uncertainty, and deliberate acceptance.

### Corrected post-Challenge admission test

1. **Claim** — Is the exact claim clear, materially non-misleading, and separated from claims with materially different authority or support?
2. **Use** — What intended or reasonably foreseeable reliance and consequence justify admission and the required review depth?
3. **Evidence** — Can authorized reviewers retrieve or legitimately reverify the supporting evidence?
4. **Support** — Does the evidence support the complete claim, including its qualifications, rather than only fragments or adjacent propositions?
5. **Authority and provenance** — What does each source legitimately establish, what reasoning transforms the evidence, and what part, if any, is Spark's internal choice?
6. **Applicability** — Are the material jurisdiction, actor, product, channel, transaction, population, audience, conditions, and exclusions clear?
7. **Currency** — Is present currency supported proportionately, with effective or as-of information, conflicts, and access limitations addressed?
8. **Uncertainty and acceptance** — Are material uncertainty, contrary evidence, exceptions, and unresolved professional, legal, privacy, confidentiality, or access constraints visible, and is `admit`, `narrow`, `hold`, `unresolved`, or `reject` authorized and justified?
9. **Reconsideration** — What evidence, event, scope, use, conflict, dependency change, or justified date requires review, and what downstream categories may be materially affected?

### Corrected post-Challenge information minimum

1. **Claim identity** — stable human-readable identifier and exact accepted wording.
2. **Purpose and scope** — intended reliance, material consequence, applicability, conditions, and exclusions.
3. **Nature and authority** — knowledge class; direct fact, synthesis, interpretation, or internal choice; legitimate external authority and Spark authority kept distinct.
4. **Evidence linkage** — precise source-record references, locations, access status, verification status, and currency information.
5. **Support and limits** — evidence relationship, complete-support conclusion, material inference, uncertainty, disagreement, exceptions, and what remains unestablished.
6. **Admission record** — disposition, rationale, decision date, acceptor, required qualified review, and unresolved professional, legal, privacy, confidentiality, or access constraints.
7. **Reconsideration** — event- or date-based triggers, related Canon, and categories of downstream materials that may require review without requiring a brittle dependency map.

The nine-question admission test and seven-group information minimum are conceptually coherent, proportionate, and consistent with accepted Spark OS Philosophy, Glossary, contribution rules, Canon boundaries, and AI Governance.

### Final audited admission test

A proposed claim or instruction may proceed to deliberate Canon acceptance only when an authorized reviewer can answer these questions proportionately to its intended and reasonably foreseeable consequences:

1. **Claim** — Is the exact claim or instruction clear, materially non-misleading, and separated from content with materially different authority, support, scope, currency, uncertainty, or disposition?
2. **Use** — What intended or reasonably foreseeable reliance and consequence justify Canon admission and the required review depth?
3. **Basis retrieval** — Can authorized reviewers retrieve or legitimately reverify the evidence, source material, or decision provenance appropriate to this knowledge class?
4. **Support or establishment** — For an evidence-based claim, does the evidence support the complete wording and qualifications rather than fragments or adjacent propositions? For a Spark choice, does an authorized decision establish the exact choice without misrepresenting it as an external requirement or evidentiary finding?
5. **Authority, provenance, and owner** — What does each source legitimately establish, what reasoning transforms the basis, what part is Spark's internal choice, who may accept that representation, and is Canon the correct repository owner?
6. **Applicability** — Are the material jurisdiction, actor, product, channel, transaction, population, audience, conditions, and exclusions clear without demanding irrelevant exhaustive metadata?
7. **Currency** — What establishes present currency for the intended use, including applicable effective, version, as-of, amendment, or supersession information, and what temporal limitation remains?
8. **Uncertainty and disposition** — Are material uncertainty, contrary evidence, disagreement, exceptions, and unresolved obligations or access constraints visible? Do they permit authorized admission of the stated or narrowed claim, or require `hold`, `unresolved`, or `reject` outside active Canon?
9. **Reconsideration** — What evidence, event, scope, use, conflict, dependency change, or justified date should trigger review, and what categories of downstream reliance may be materially affected?

#### Admission-result boundary

- `Admit` means the exact reviewed claim may proceed to deliberate human acceptance and canonical installation.
- `Narrow` means revise the claim and test the narrowed wording before admission; narrowing alone does not make it Canon.
- `Hold` means potentially useful but not presently ready.
- `Unresolved` means the available basis does not justify a settled Spark position.
- `Reject` means the proposed claim should not be admitted in its tested form.

Only an explicitly accepted and correctly installed `admit` result becomes active Canon. Review status, confidence, source quality, practitioner involvement, or repository presence does not substitute for acceptance.

### Final audited information minimum

Seven information groups must remain recoverable in a form proportionate to the claim. They are not seven mandatory fields and need not occupy one physical record.

1. **Claim identity** — a stable human-readable reference and the exact accepted wording.
2. **Purpose and scope** — intended or reasonably foreseeable reliance, material consequence, applicability, conditions, and exclusions needed to prevent misuse.
3. **Nature, authority, and owner** — whether the content is direct fact, synthesis, interpretation, procedure, or internal choice; the legitimate external authority and Spark acceptance authority; and why Canon is the correct repository owner.
4. **Basis linkage** — precise references to retrievable evidence, source material, or decision provenance, including location, access, verification, and relevant currency information.
5. **Support and limits** — how the basis supports or establishes the complete claim, material reasoning transformations, uncertainty, contrary evidence, disagreement, exceptions, and what remains unestablished.
6. **Admission record** — disposition, rationale, decision date, acceptor, required qualified review, and confirmation that any remaining limitations are compatible with the authorized scope. Blocking professional, legal, privacy, confidentiality, access, or authority constraints require non-admission until resolved or the claim is validly narrowed.
7. **Reconsideration** — material event- or date-based triggers, related Canon, and categories of downstream material that may require review, without requiring a complete manually maintained dependency map.

#### Information-surface boundary

These groups describe required recoverability, not file architecture:

- a Canon document may satisfy shared purpose, authority, scope, or source information once for several compatible claims;
- a reusable source citation or record may support several claims without duplicating publication and access metadata;
- an admission record may be a section, linked review artifact, accepted decision record, or future repository structure;
- Git history and a pull request may support provenance but do not by themselves explain claim support, scope, or uncertainty; and
- `not applicable` is acceptable when justified rather than used to avoid a material question.

### Proportionality test

The architecture works across consequence levels because the information obligation is stable while review depth varies.

For a stable, low-consequence internal reference, shared document-level context, concise decision provenance, and an event-based trigger may be sufficient.

For volatile, regulated, client-facing, or high-consequence knowledge, responsible admission may require claim-specific wording, official or otherwise legitimate authority, precise applicability, effective/version evidence, qualified review, visible uncertainty, and reliable reconsideration.

Proportionality does not permit omission of a factor that could materially change meaning or safe use. It also does not require exhaustive metadata that cannot affect the admission decision.

### Circularity and gaming audit

The final structure rejects the following shortcuts:

- accepted because it appears in Canon;
- authoritative because a source supports an accepted claim;
- current because it was recently retrieved;
- applicable because it is used operationally;
- supported because several sources are listed;
- verified because a link resolves;
- professionally established because a practitioner stated it;
- safe because a generic caveat is present;
- reviewed because a calendar date was updated;
- accountable because a name appears in metadata; and
- independently verified because AI output was reformatted or repeated by another tool.
