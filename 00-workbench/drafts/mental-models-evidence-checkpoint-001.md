## Candidate 01 — Representation Is Not Capability

### Practical observations

The prototype and its review repeatedly preserved the distinction that:

- course completion does not establish competence;
- a correct answer does not establish contextual judgment;
- a polished course or source map does not establish authoritative domain knowledge;
- accurate AI organization cannot substitute for a current licensed practitioner's operational judgment;
- and documentation can support capability development without producing it.

The practitioner-review boundary also reserves substantive operational validation to a current licensed professional and limits AI to organization or wording.

### What this evidence establishes

The candidate's language is coherent and transferable to a real course-development context. It helped preserve a clean boundary between a learning artifact and the human capability that might eventually be developed or assessed through broader practice.

The evidence also confirms the candidate's limits:

- reasonable reliance on courses, assessments, credentials, procedures, and AI remains legitimate within scope;
- authority, currency, factual reliability, and legal permission are separate questions;
- and observed performance remains evidence rather than a representation.

### What this evidence does not establish

The boundary was already explicit in the accepted Manifesto and in the instructions used to create and audit the prototype. Its activation was therefore prompted rather than naturally observed.

No actual learner, hiring, promotion, delegation, client-understanding, or readiness decision changed because someone independently activated the model. The prototype did not test whether the model improves judgment beyond Principles 1, 5, and 6 or whether its terminology is natural in everyday use.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate alignment evidence; weak independent-use evidence.**

This is stronger than a hypothetical example but remains one bounded, Philosophy-informed process rather than repeated Spark Mortgages use.

### Disposition

**Continue observation before Distill.**

Candidate 01 remains non-authoritative Mental Model source material. It is not accepted, rejected, renamed, or installed. Future evidence should come from a real preparedness, responsibility, learning, or client-understanding judgment in which the model adds something identifiable beyond the accepted Principles.

## Candidate 02 — What Changes When We Simplify?

### Practical observations

Course Prototype 001 compressed several distinct mortgage questions into the labels `insured`, `insurable`, and `uninsurable`.

That simplification made important distinctions harder to notice, including:

- LTV versus actual insurance status;
- insurance eligibility versus actual insurer or product fit;
- published insurer eligibility versus lender-panel and channel availability;
- occupancy versus property type and unit count;
- transactional versus portfolio or bulk insurance;
- premium responsibility versus insurance status;
- and externally researchable facts versus Spark policy choices.

The simplified taxonomy then produced memorable but materially overbroad answer keys. The Source Map also made consequential derived claims look like fictional scenario content, and general warnings did not adequately constrain the categorical answer surfaces.

Applying a transformation-focused review changed the successor direction from a three-label taxonomy to nine separate decision dimensions used through a candidate decision sequence. The review also retained the possibility that tightly defined shorthand could remain useful after the dimensions are established.

### What this evidence establishes

The model's central questions produced a useful and inspectable result:

- What became easier to notice?
- What became harder to notice?
- Could the change alter responsible interpretation or action?
- What must be visible now, and what can remain accessibly layered?

The case demonstrates both harmful and positive transformation. The original simplification obscured material distinctions; the proposed dimensions and sequence may improve visibility without requiring every detail in the first layer.

### What this evidence does not establish

The review was deliberately prompted to test simplification and was conducted on a course artifact. It does not demonstrate natural activation across ordinary Spark decisions, dashboards, client explanations, handoffs, or operating procedures.

Some defects were discovered through primary-source verification and domain expertise rather than the Mental Model itself. The model evaluates transformation; it does not establish that a source is true, current, authoritative, complete, legal, or operationally available.

One strong content-design case does not establish repeated cross-domain Philosophy value.

### Evidence strength

**Strong single-case usefulness; weak repeated-use and Philosophy-ownership evidence.**

The model clearly improved a real artifact review. The same evidence also strengthens the pre-existing concern that its natural owner is Academy, editorial practice, or content-transformation review.

### Disposition

**Provisionally reclassify downstream.**

Preserve Candidate 02 as non-authoritative source material for a future Academy or content-transformation design standard. Do not install it in Academy yet and do not discard its audited limits.

Future use must preserve:

- a materiality or consequence threshold;
- positive simplification as well as meaning-loss detection;
- audience and purpose dependence;
- responsibly accessible layered context;
- and the separation of transformation fidelity from truth, currency, authority, legality, and reliability.

Candidate 02 no longer remains an active candidate for foundational Mental Models Distill unless later non-editorial Spark experience demonstrates that the same mechanism repeatedly improves broader organizational judgment.
