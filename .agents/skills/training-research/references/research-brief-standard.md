# Research Brief Standard — Pilot 001

## Purpose

Apply this standard to the single research brief created by Training Research Pilot 001.

The brief records instructional-design research evidence. It is not authoritative Spark OS knowledge, Canon, accepted Academy content, or an Academy design decision.

The standard governs evidence capture and presentation only. It does not establish a Canon sourcing standard, Academy acceptance standard, course-development protocol, supersession process, retirement process, or automatic downstream action.

## Fixed Pilot 001 inputs

Use exactly:

### Research lane

`instructional-design`

### Research question

What does current research indicate about the effects, limitations, and conditions of worked examples for novice learners in professional or comparable educational settings, and what transfer limitations apply when considering mortgage-broker training?

### Topic slug

`worked-examples-novice-professional-learning`

### Intended use

Test whether a bounded research brief can provide traceable instructional-design evidence for later Course Prototype review without becoming an Academy decision.

### Scope

Include evidence about worked examples for novice learning in professional or sufficiently comparable educational settings.

Examine:

- positive findings;
- null findings;
- adverse findings;
- boundary conditions;
- limitations;
- learner context;
- instructional context;
- disagreement;
- evidence strength;
- context dependence;
- transfer concerns.

Do not assume that worked examples improve learning.

### Excluded scope

Exclude:

- mortgage-domain facts;
- legal or regulatory claims;
- lender or insurer information;
- mortgage products;
- underwriting;
- qualification;
- operations;
- creation or revision of course content;
- Academy acceptance;
- Canon review;
- recommendations to accepted Philosophy.

### Jurisdiction

`not-applicable` for authority purposes.

Record the geographic, institutional, professional, educational, and learner context of each source when relevant.

### Source boundary

Use publicly accessible sources only.

Do not use:

- access-controlled sources;
- subscription sources;
- confidential information;
- proprietary material;
- client information;
- lender or insurer materials;
- sources requiring unauthorized access.

### Source quality

Prioritize:

- peer-reviewed systematic reviews;
- meta-analyses;
- scholarly syntheses;
- original empirical research.

Seek at least one credible scholarly synthesis and one independent scholarly source where available.

Source count does not substitute for relevance, methodological quality, applicability, or independence.

If adequate evidence is unavailable, produce a properly bounded `limited` or `failed` brief.

## Research status

Assign exactly one:

- `completed`: The bounded question received a reasonably supported answer under this standard.
- `limited`: Useful evidence was collected, but material gaps, access limitations, contextual limits, or unresolved uncertainty remain.
- `failed`: The invocation could not produce usable evidence safely or reliably.

`Completed` does not mean authoritative, accepted, approved, ready to teach, or incorporated into Spark OS.

A correctly bounded `limited` or `failed` result may still demonstrate that the skill operated successfully.

## Required brief structure

Use the following headings and fields in this order.

# Training Research Brief

## Status notice

Include this notice:

> This brief contains instructional-design research evidence collected for Pilot 001. It is not authoritative Spark OS knowledge, Canon, accepted Academy content, or an Academy design decision. It may not be incorporated automatically into course content or used to establish a teachable Spark fact.

## Brief identification

- **Research status:** `completed`, `limited`, or `failed`
- **Research lane:** `instructional-design`
- **Research question:** Use the exact fixed question.
- **Topic slug:** `worked-examples-novice-professional-learning`
- **Research date:** `YYYY-MM-DD`
- **Filename:** Exact created filename
- **Intended use:** Use the fixed intended-use statement.

## Scope

### Included

Use the fixed scope and identify the educational settings, learner groups, and forms of worked examples actually examined.

### Excluded

Use the fixed excluded scope.

### Context

Describe relevant:

- geographic context;
- institutional context;
- professional or educational setting;
- learner experience level;
- task type;
- instructional conditions.

## Research method

State:

- how sources were sought;
- which source types were prioritized;
- date or currency considerations;
- public-access boundary;
- material search limitations;
- whether adequate scholarly synthesis and independent evidence were found.

Do not claim a systematic review unless a genuine systematic-review method was followed.

## Findings

Present the principal findings without assuming a positive effect.

Include positive, null, adverse, weak, conditional, or conflicting findings when supported.

For every material finding, identify the supporting source IDs.

## Contrary evidence and disagreement

Record:

- credible contrary evidence;
- null or adverse findings;
- disagreements among sources;
- differences in definition, learner population, setting, task, intervention, or outcome;
- unresolved conflicts;
- limits of the search for contrary evidence.

Do not manufacture consensus.

## Conditions and boundary factors

Identify conditions that may affect the evidence, including where supported:

- learner prior knowledge;
- task complexity;
- worked-example design;
- explanation quality;
- fading or transition to independent practice;
- learner self-explanation;
- instructional timing;
- assessment type;
- cognitive load;
- professional versus academic context.

Do not add unsupported conditions merely because they appear plausible.

## Transfer considerations for mortgage-broker training

Separate direct evidence from transfer inference.

Identify:

- which evidence comes from professional education;
- which evidence comes from only comparable settings;
- material differences from mortgage-broker training;
- assumptions required for transfer;
- limitations on generalization;
- what would require later Course Prototype judgment.

Do not make mortgage-domain claims or recommend an accepted Academy decision.

## Evidence classification

Include all five categories using their controlling definitions in [Evidence distinctions](#evidence-distinctions).

### Source-supported evidence

List each source-supported statement and provide its supporting source IDs.

### Synthesis

List each synthesis, identify its supporting source IDs, and explain its comparative basis.

### Inference

For each inference, state:

- the inference;
- supporting evidence;
- reasoning;
- assumptions;
- limitations.

### Researcher interpretation

State possible significance for later Course Prototype review without presenting it as a source finding or Spark OS decision.

### Uncertainty

Identify:

- missing evidence;
- weak evidence;
- contextual ambiguity;
- methodological limitations;
- access limitations;
- unresolved disagreement;
- incomplete verification;
- uncertainty about transfer.

Keep material uncertainty visible in the findings and conclusion.

## Potential relationship to named earlier briefs

If no earlier brief was explicitly named, state:

`No earlier brief was authorized for comparison.`

If an earlier brief was explicitly named and a potential conflict was identified, provide:

- exact earlier filename;
- neutral description of the potential conflict;
- relevant evidence;
- this statement:

> The relationship remains unresolved. This brief does not edit, supersede, retire, archive, or determine the authority of the earlier brief.

## Limitations

Summarize the most important limitations affecting:

- source access;
- evidence quality;
- source independence;
- population applicability;
- context;
- research currency;
- outcome measurement;
- transfer to mortgage-broker training.

## Conclusion

Answer the fixed research question proportionately to the evidence.

Do not imply that worked examples necessarily improve learning.

State:

- what the evidence supports;
- what it does not support;
- important conditions;
- important limitations;
- the level of confidence justified;
- what remains unresolved.

## Later-use boundary

Include:

> Consultation of this brief by a later Course Prototype task requires Sheldon to name its exact path explicitly. That consultation rule is a provisional Pilot 001 safeguard only. Permission to consult does not make the brief authoritative, approve its incorporation, or establish an Academy decision.

## Sources

Create one source record per cited source.

Use stable source IDs:

- `S01`
- `S02`
- `S03`

Do not assign a source ID to an inaccessible search result unless it is clearly marked as an unverified lead and is not used to support a finding.

## Required source metadata

Record for every source:

- **Source ID**
- **Title**
- **Author or responsible organization**
- **Source type**
- **Publication date:** exact date, partial date, or `not stated`
- **Access date**
- **URL, DOI, stable identifier, or precise source location**
- **Access status**
- **Verification status**
- **Relevance**
- **Material limitations**

Do not infer a publication date from a webpage footer, copyright year, search-result date, or unrelated page metadata.

## Conditional source metadata

Record when relevant or available:

- **Publisher or issuing body:** when different from the author
- **Effective date**
- **Jurisdiction**
- **Exact page, section, paragraph, table, or heading**
- **Copyright or licensing information**
- **Access or preservation restrictions**

Use `not applicable`, `not stated`, or `not established` when that distinction is material.

## Evidence distinctions

### Source-supported evidence

A statement directly supported by a cited source.

### Synthesis

A conclusion drawn by comparing multiple cited sources.

### Inference

A reasoned extension beyond what the sources directly establish.

### Researcher interpretation

An explanation of possible relevance to later Spark review.

### Uncertainty

A limitation arising from missing, weak, inaccessible, ambiguous, conflicting, context-dependent, or incompletely verified evidence.

Do not blend these categories.

## Source-access handling

Check permitted public research access.

Record every material access or verification limitation accurately.

### Inaccessible source

An inaccessible source may be recorded as an unverified lead but may not support a finding.

Record:

- identity, if known;
- attempted access;
- access limitation;
- verification status;
- whether an accessible alternative was found.

Do not represent a search snippet, citation fragment, abstract-only record, or inaccessible page as if the complete source was verified.

### Unverifiable source

Label the source `unverified`, do not rely on it for a material finding, and seek a verifiable alternative where practical.

### Irreconcilable sources

Present each credible position, its evidence, and the unresolved disagreement.

Do not select a conclusion without adequate support.

### Inadequate evidence

Use `limited` or `failed` status rather than padding the brief with weak or fabricated claims.

## Quotation and summary rules

Summarization is the default.

Write summaries in original language, preserve qualifications, and cite the supporting source.

Use a short quotation only when exact wording is materially important and the quotation:

- is no longer than necessary;
- is properly attributed;
- complies with applicable copyright, licensing, contractual, and platform restrictions;
- contains no sensitive or protected information.

Do not reproduce substantial portions of a source.

## Storage boundary

Store only the Markdown research brief.

The brief may contain:

- citations;
- stable links;
- original summaries;
- compliant short quotations.

Do not store:

- downloaded articles;
- PDFs;
- screenshots;
- datasets;
- webpage copies;
- source archives;
- subscription content;
- licensed training materials;
- proprietary documents;
- confidential information;
- client information.

## Copyright and licensing

Do not:

- bypass paywalls or access controls;
- assume public access permits unrestricted reuse;
- reproduce substantial copyrighted material;
- remove copyright or attribution notices;
- misrepresent licence terms;
- preserve material when reuse rights are unclear.

When rights are unclear, link to the source and summarize conservatively.

## Privacy and restricted information

Do not include:

- personal or client information;
- application details;
- financial account information;
- credentials;
- confidential communications;
- proprietary lender or insurer information;
- access-controlled material;
- information obtained without authorization.

If prohibited information appears, stop processing it, exclude it from the brief, and report the boundary issue without repeating the protected content.

## Non-authority and teachable-fact boundary

Collected evidence does not become authoritative because it is credible, recent, peer reviewed, official, frequently cited, or useful.

Pilot 001 evidence may inform later Course Prototype review only when the exact brief is explicitly named.

Instructional-design evidence cannot become an Academy design decision without a separate Academy review and acceptance process.

Pilot 001 does not authorize domain research. Any future domain evidence would require separate review and could not become a teachable Spark fact without an applicable Canon review and acceptance path.

## Pilot access-validation rule

Validate the pilot using this rule:

> Permitted public research access was checked, and any access or verification limitation was recorded accurately.

Do not require that adequate public access was available for the pilot to be handled correctly.

A correctly handled `limited` or `failed` result may still constitute a successful skill pilot.
