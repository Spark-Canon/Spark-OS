# Minimum Canon Sourcing and Entry Standard Exploration 001

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Archive status:** Historical reference only
**Authority:** Non-authoritative conversation summary

> Repository documents are authoritative. Archived conversations are historical reference only.

## Purpose

This conversation explored the smallest sourcing and admission architecture that could safely govern future claims entering Spark OS Canon, challenged it against difficult evidence cases, corrected a material source-traceability defect, and closed the audited result as non-authoritative Workbench source material.

The work deliberately avoided drafting mortgage Canon, Academy content, a comprehensive schema, an authoritative standard, or an installation package.

## Context

Spark OS already distinguished Workbench exploration from accepted knowledge, Canon from Academy derivation, external source authority from repository acceptance, evidence from judgment and authority, and AI output from accepted Spark OS authority.

Current Canon guidance required accepted material to be accurate, clearly scoped, internally consistent, traceable when facts may change, reusable, explicit about uncertainty and exceptions, and separated from temporary teaching activities.

Course Prototype 001 had exposed sourcing and simplification failures. Its claim inventory and mortgage taxonomy remained non-authoritative evidence suitable for testing architecture, not establishing current mortgage facts or Canon.

The unresolved question was whether Spark OS needed a small, proportionate Canon sourcing and entry standard before attempting a bounded Canon pilot.

## Major Milestones

### Representative Claim Exploration

The exploration tested five materially different claim types:

1. an external regulatory or legal claim
2. an insurer policy claim
3. a proposed Spark internal practice
4. a professional interpretation
5. a simplified teaching statement derived from another claim

The cases showed that source presence alone was insufficient. Responsible admission also required clarity about:

- the exact proposition
- intended reliance
- source retrievability
- complete evidentiary support
- legitimate source authority
- jurisdiction and operational applicability
- effective or as-of context
- whether the representation was fact, synthesis, inference, interpretation, or internal choice
- material uncertainty and disagreement
- reconsideration triggers
- deliberate admission disposition

A stale official source demonstrated that official authorship and retrievability do not establish current validity. Restricted lender evidence demonstrated that public access cannot be universally required, while authorized reverification and honest access classification remain necessary. A teaching simplification demonstrated that Academy compression can create a broader claim than the source supports.

The controlling Explore revision produced nine provisional admission questions:

2. Intended reliance
3. Knowledge class and authority
4. Evidence support
5. Scope and applicability

7. Uncertainty and contrary evidence
8. Currency
9. Acceptance

It also produced nine provisional entry fields:

5. Scope and applicability

7. Uncertainty and exceptions
8. Currency and review trigger

These were exploratory information requirements rather than an accepted template, schema, or repository layer.

### Focused Challenge

The emerging architecture was tested against:

- external authority versus Spark repository authority
- stale official sources
- access-controlled lender, insurer, vendor, and proprietary material
- practitioner observation and disagreement
- Spark practices stricter than external minimums
- direct support, synthesis, inference, interpretation, and internal choice
- plausible AI synthesis without independently reviewable support
- documents containing mixed-status claims
- entry-, document-, source-, and review-level information
- stable low-consequence knowledge and volatile high-consequence knowledge
- event-based reconsideration and fixed expiry
- downstream dependency awareness
- metadata-created false confidence

The Challenge produced useful substantive findings and a proposed compression into nine admission questions and seven information groups. It also incorrectly reconstructed the controlling source as twelve questions and ten fields.

### Source-Mismatch Objection

The mismatch was identified as a material traceability defect. Strong substantive reasoning could not justify item-level dispositions against a source structure that did not exist.

Formal Audit was withheld until traceability to the actual nine-question/nine-field source was restored. A complete Challenge rerun was rejected as unnecessary because the edge-case reasoning remained useful; the procedural defect could be isolated and corrected.

### Bounded Traceability Correction

The correction mapped every controlling question and field into the proposed final architecture.

Key mappings included:

- Evidence support separated into retrieval and complete-support tests.
- Basis of judgment was distributed across authority, provenance, and support.
- Currency separated present validity from future reconsideration.
- Acceptance remained visible through legitimate authority, admission disposition, constraints, and access treatment.
- Entry name and Accepted statement combined under claim identity.
- Knowledge class and Authority became nature, authority, and admission provenance.
- Basis and provenance divided across basis linkage and support analysis.
- Currency and review trigger separated source currency from reconsideration.
- Acceptance and dependencies divided between admission records and downstream reconsideration.

No material safeguard was removed, and Formal Audit was authorized.

### Formal Audit

Formal Audit passed the corrected architecture with bounded refinements.

The final audited nine admission questions were:

1. Claim
2. Use
3. Basis retrieval
4. Support or establishment
5. Authority, provenance, and owner
6. Applicability
7. Currency
8. Uncertainty and disposition
9. Reconsideration

The final seven recoverable information groups were:

1. Claim identity
2. Purpose and scope
3. Nature, authority, and owner
4. Basis linkage
5. Support and limits
6. Admission record
7. Reconsideration

Formal Audit added several durable boundaries:

- Spark choices and procedures require authorized internal decision provenance rather than fictional external proof.
- Only a deliberately accepted and correctly installed `admit` result may enter active Canon.
- `Narrow`, `hold`, `unresolved`, and `reject` are legitimate outcomes but do not create Canon.
- Source and admission records are logical information functions, not mandatory files, registries, schemas, or repository layers.
- Materially distinct claims must remain independently reviewable without requiring one file or metadata block per sentence.
- Canon fit must prevent Governance rules, Academy derivation, Workbench evidence, and external authority from being misallocated into Canon.

The information requirements remained stable while review depth varied proportionately with volatility, consequence, obligations, and foreseeable reliance.

### Exploration Close

The exploration closed on 2026-08-17 as completed non-authoritative source material.

Close preserved the audited nine-question test, seven information groups, admission-result boundary, logical information-surface boundary, authority allocation, proportionality protections, smallest-owner test, reopening conditions, and decisions reserved for Sheldon Phillips.

No unresolved conceptual, authority, evidence, scope, currency, allocation, maintenance, proportionality, or gaming defect required reopening before Distill.

Closure did not approve, install, activate, operationalize, or authorize the proposed standard.

## Alternatives and Concerns

The exploration rejected or deferred:

- a comprehensive mortgage ontology or Canon schema
- universal source-count requirements
- a universal primary-source ritual
- a flat hierarchy such as “insurer always wins”
- mandatory public URLs for legitimate restricted evidence
- confidence scores
- universal expiry periods
- a comprehensive manually maintained downstream dependency map
- mandatory source-record and admission-record files
- one-file-per-claim atomization
- metadata completeness as a substitute for substantive review
- immediate use of Course Prototype C04 as a Canon pilot
- beginning Distill during Close
- rerunning the entire Focused Challenge after the traceability defect

The central risks were false confidence from metadata, invented provenance for restricted evidence, conflating source authority with repository acceptance, hiding internal Spark choices behind external sources, and allowing Academy simplification or AI synthesis to create unsupported domain truth.

## Architectural Principles Discussed

- The claim, not necessarily the whole document, is the primary Canon-admission unit.
- Source authority is proposition-specific rather than globally hierarchical.
- External authority, Spark repository authority, and operational applicability are distinct.
- Evidence retrieval and complete support prevent different failures.
- Current validity and future reconsideration are distinct tests.
- Fact, synthesis, inference, professional interpretation, and internal choice require different provenance.
- Internal choices require legitimate decision provenance rather than fabricated external proof.
- Restricted evidence requires protected, authorized, reproducible review without invented provenance.
- Academy simplification cannot create the domain truth it teaches.
- Only explicitly accepted and correctly installed admitted knowledge becomes active Canon.
- Logical information functions should not become repository layers without demonstrated need.
- Review depth may vary proportionately while essential admission questions remain stable.
- Closing an exploration preserves tested reasoning without creating acceptance.

These principles are historical observations unless confirmed by the authoritative documents linked below.

## Outcome

The conversation produced a closed, audited, non-authoritative architecture for a future minimum Canon sourcing and entry standard:

- nine admission questions
- seven recoverable information groups
- explicit admission dispositions
- separation of source, admission, Governance, Canon, Academy, and downstream responsibilities
- proportional review and gaming protections
- a smallest-owner test favoring initial fit assessment within the existing Canon overview before considering a separate standards document

The local exploration, task notepad, and foundational audit were updated outside current `main`, and local integrity and formatting checks reportedly passed. Those Workbench artifacts remain non-authoritative and are not linked from this summary because they were not installed in the repository baseline used for archival publication.

No pull request, commit, merge, Canon entry, Academy content, schema, ADR, research invocation, pilot, authoritative acceptance, or canonical installation resulted from the summarized conversation.

## Lessons Learned

- Challenge must remain traceable to the exact controlling source architecture.
- A bounded correction is preferable to full rework when the defect can be isolated without discarding valid findings.
- Source authority must be evaluated relative to the proposition being supported.
- Retrieval and complete support should remain separate tests.
- Currency requires more than recent retrieval.
- Reconsideration should respond to volatility, consequence, and discoverability rather than one universal expiry.
- Restricted evidence requires honest access handling rather than mandatory publication or unverifiable memory.
- Claim-level reviewability does not require excessive file atomization.
- Metadata assists judgment but cannot replace substantive review.
- Rigour can be preserved by varying review depth rather than omitting essential questions.
